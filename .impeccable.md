## Design Context

### Users
The primary user is a **church volunteer on a phone**: not technical, often older or busy, using GatherFlow on a Sunday or in short gaps during the week. Their job is small and concrete: welcome a newcomer, make a follow-up call or message, record what happened, check someone in. Staff (administrators, pastors, follow-up coordinators) use the same app on laptops for setup, assigning work and reports, but the volunteer's phone flow sets the bar for simplicity.

The data is about people and their spiritual and pastoral lives, so it is sensitive. Every screen must make it obvious what the person is allowed to see and do, without making them feel policed.

### Brand Personality
**Warm, calm, human.** It should feel like a caring helper sitting beside you, not software or a CRM. Plain words over jargon ("people who visited" rather than "pipeline"), encouragement over urgency, and quiet confidence that nothing will be lost. The emotional goals are reassurance ("I know what to do next"), belonging ("this is for our church"), and gentle accomplishment when work is done.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** light by default, with dark mode following the device setting.
- **Tone:** warm, soft and spacious. Tinted warm neutrals instead of grey, one warm, confident accent, generous spacing and large readable type. Few borders and cards; use space and type for hierarchy.
- **Anti-references:** generic corporate SaaS dashboards (grey shadcn defaults, metric-tile grids, dense tables as the first thing you see), and churchy clichés (stained glass, crosses, gothic or script fonts, purple and gold).
- **Current state, to be replaced:** stock shadcn neutral grayscale, Geist font, no brand assets.

### Design Principles
1. **One next step.** Every screen leads with the single most likely action for this person; everything else is secondary or behind disclosure.
2. **Phone first, thumb first.** Design for a narrow screen and one hand: primary actions reachable at the bottom, large tap targets, no hover-only affordances. The desktop layout is a widening of the phone layout, not the other way round.
3. **Say it like a person.** Plain, kind language. No internal terms, codes or jargon in the primary flow; status explained in words.
4. **Calm over busy.** Fewer containers, fewer numbers, more space. Show what needs doing, not everything that exists.
5. **Role shapes the app.** A volunteer sees their own work and little else; staff see more. The navigation should feel small for whoever is holding it.
